Audit methodology and limitations

Understand what we check, how scores work, and why evidence matters more than an isolated number.

Updated September 26, 2026 · MandAPI GEO team

What does the audit cover?

MandAPI GEO reads the public HTML of one URL and checks supporting site files such as robots.txt, a sitemap, and llms.txt when available. The report groups technical SEO and AI search readiness checks.

We do not execute JavaScript, crawl every page, or access authenticated areas. Content displayed only after scripts run may not appear in the report.

How are scores calculated?

Each eligible check has a weight. A passing check earns 100% of its weight; a warning earns 50%; a failure earns 0%. Informational checks do not affect the score.

FAQ patterns, numbers, first-paragraph length, lists, tables, and organization markup are informational observations, with no penalty for absence. Training preferences and llms.txt do not change the score either. Do not add these elements simply to raise a score.

Area score = earned points ÷ eligible weights × 100, rounded. The overall score is an equally weighted average of SEO and AI readiness. It is not the probability of indexing or citation.

A high score means that the page meets more of this tool’s criteria. It does not measure Google rankings, actual ChatGPT mentions, traffic, conversions, or complete editorial quality.

How should I read priorities?

Findings are ordered by severity and then by estimated rule impact. Start with technical barriers, inspect the observed value, and decide whether each recommendation applies to your page. Severity guides review; it does not estimate financial loss.

Expand each check to inspect evidence and guidance. A detail missing from the fetched HTML may exist in other versions of the page.

What does AI readiness mean?

We evaluate observable signals such as content structure, identification, and crawler access rules. These checks do not reproduce search engines’ internal systems. The audit does not directly test answers generated by assistants.

Files such as llms.txt and special structured data are not universal GEO requirements. A file’s presence does not prove that a search engine uses it. Read the guide and its sources.

What can affect the results?

  • A report captures an HTTP response at one moment. Sites may vary by region, device, cookies, or bot protection.
  • An available sitemap does not confirm that its URLs are indexed.
  • robots.txt describes access rules; it does not prove actual crawling, training, or citations.
  • We do not measure Core Web Vitals, backlinks, authority, search volumes, or full accessibility.
  • Heuristic checks may produce false positives or miss issues.

How can I track improvements?

Download the full report, record your changes, and audit the same URL again. Combine findings with human review and real search data. Compare results only when the URL, content, and conditions are comparable.

What does a comparison confirm?

A baseline is a compact copy of an earlier report. Comparison requires the same requested and final URLs, compatible report, engine, and scoring versions, and a newer audit date. Score differences appear only when coverage and weights also match.

An issue is marked resolved only when the same check later passes. A missing, unknown, or informational result does not confirm a fix. Worsened issues are also included among persistent issues, so those category totals should not be added together. Observed changes do not prove causality or better rankings and citations.

Older reports without version information can be imported as baselines but do not produce confirmed comparisons. Imported files are validated locally; this does not authenticate their data source. Keep copies of real reports for review.
