A practical GEO guide: start with the basics

A path to better SEO and content readiness for AI search, without shortcuts or promises of citations.

Updated September 26, 2026 · MandAPI GEO team

What is GEO?

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization: practices intended to make content easier to find and understand in AI search experiences. A useful starting point is technical access, clear answers, and trustworthy information.

This is practical guidance from MandAPI GEO. There is no formula that guarantees inclusion in assistant responses.

1. Make sure the page can be read

Check that the public URL returns useful HTML, has a specific title, and contains no accidental indexing blocks. Check that internal links point to the correct page version and that sitemap entries use canonical URLs.

Exercise: open the page source and look for its main answer. If it only appears after JavaScript runs, this tool may miss it. Review rendering with suitable tools.

2. Answer first; add detail afterward

Choose a real reader need. Answer directly, then add examples, conditions, and limitations. Use descriptive headings and lists or tables where they simplify comparison.

Exercise: ask someone unfamiliar with your business to explain your service after reading the first paragraph. Rewrite anything ambiguous.

3. Provide verifiable context

Identify the person or organization responsible where relevant. Describe real experience and methods, cite primary sources, and update information that changes. Dates, numbers, and credentials need a basis; do not add them just to satisfy an automated rule.

4. Avoid GEO shortcuts

Google recommends SEO fundamentals for its AI features and does not require special schema for them. Its documentation says llms.txt neither helps nor harms Google visibility. It cannot replace accessible pages and useful content. Source: Google Search Central.

Use structured data appropriate to visible content. Do not invent reviews, questions, prices, or authorship to fill a markup template.

5. Separate search from training

OpenAI documents different purposes: OAI-SearchBot relates to search, while GPTBot relates to training. Review each agent’s rules according to your publishing policy; allowing training is not a requirement for search inclusion. Access also does not guarantee citation. Source: OpenAI bot documentation.

6. Measure what actually happened

Use this report to identify actions. Evaluate outcomes with indexing, query, traffic, and conversion data from your actual tools. A score change does not prove increased visibility or establish that a single change caused an outcome.

Review checklist: accessible page, clear title, useful answer, identifiable responsibility when relevant, verifiable sources, and deliberate crawler policies. Improve quality, then compare real outcomes.
